Activity 1A

Case-Based Learning (CBL): Applying Community Health Nursing Concepts in Family Care

The Case-Based Learning (CBL) activity involved examining and analyzing a family health case to apply the fundamental concepts and principles of Community Health Nursing. Through the given situation, the group explored the family’s health conditions, identified existing and potential health concerns, and considered the factors influencing its overall well-being. The activity provided an opportunity to connect theoretical nursing concepts with realistic family healthcare situations.

Purpose of the Activity

  • To understand the family as the basic unit of care in Community Health Nursing.
  • To identify and analyze existing and potential family health problems.
  • To recognize the biological, psychological, social, cultural, and environmental factors affecting family health.
  • To apply appropriate nursing principles in assessing family needs and developing interventions.
  • To strengthen clinical reasoning and decision-making in providing family-centered care.

Community Health Nursing Concepts Demonstrated

  • Family as the Unit of Care: Recognizing the family as an essential focus of nursing assessment and intervention.
  • Holistic Family Assessment: Considering the physical, psychological, social, cultural, and environmental factors influencing health.
  • Health Promotion and Disease Prevention: Identifying opportunities to improve health practices and minimize potential risks.
  • Family Nursing Process: Applying systematic assessment, identification of health problems, planning, implementation, and evaluation.
  • Family Participation and Empowerment: Encouraging family members to become active participants in healthcare decisions and health maintenance.

Skills and Competencies Developed

  • Critical thinking and clinical reasoning in analyzing family health situations.
  • Assessment and identification of family health needs and risk factors.
  • Recognition and prioritization of health problems.
  • Development of appropriate and individualized nursing interventions.
  • Application of theoretical knowledge to practical healthcare situations.
  • Collaboration and effective communication during case analysis.

Application to Community Nursing Practice

The activity emphasized the importance of understanding the family’s overall situation rather than focusing solely on an individual’s health condition. In actual community nursing practice, this knowledge can be applied through comprehensive family assessments, identification of health risks, and development of appropriate nursing interventions. It also reinforces the importance of considering family dynamics, available resources, and environmental conditions when providing care. Through these approaches, community health nurses can promote preventive healthcare, strengthen family participation, and improve the overall health and well-being of families.

Activity 1B

Problem-Based Learning (PBL) Title: How Can We Improve the Health of the Family?

The Problem-Based Learning (PBL) activity focused on identifying and addressing family health concerns through collaborative problem-solving and critical analysis. The group examined a presented health situation, explored its underlying causes and contributing factors, and formulated appropriate strategies to improve the family’s health. The activity encouraged the application of Community Health Nursing principles, available healthcare resources, and evidence-based information in developing practical solutions.

Purpose of the Activity

  • To recognize and analyze common health concerns affecting families.
  • To identify contributing factors, health risks, and barriers to accessing healthcare.
  • To establish priorities based on the family’s identified health needs.
  • To formulate appropriate, realistic, and evidence-based nursing interventions.
  • To encourage family participation and responsibility in maintaining health.
  • To develop problem-solving, collaboration, and decision-making skills.

Community Health Nursing Concepts Demonstrated

  • Family Nursing Process: Utilizing a systematic approach to assess, identify, plan, implement, and evaluate interventions for family health concerns.
  • Primary Health Care: Promoting accessible, appropriate, and community-oriented healthcare services.
  • Health Promotion and Disease Prevention: Encouraging healthy practices and preventive measures to reduce health risks.
  • Health Education: Providing relevant information to improve the family’s knowledge and healthcare practices.
  • Community Resources and Participation: Recognizing the importance of healthcare facilities, local health workers, and community support systems.
  • Evidence-Based Practice: Utilizing reliable health information and research to support nursing decisions and interventions.

Skills and Competencies Developed

  • Problem identification, analysis, and prioritization.
  • Critical thinking and evidence-based decision-making.
  • Data gathering and interpretation.
  • Nursing intervention planning and problem-solving.
  • Effective communication and collaborative teamwork.
  • Recognition and utilization of available healthcare and community resources.

Application to Community Nursing Practice

The activity demonstrated how community health nurses can address family health concerns through systematic assessment, appropriate prioritization, and practical intervention planning. In community settings, nurses must consider not only the immediate health problems of families but also their underlying causes, socioeconomic circumstances, health practices, and access to healthcare services. The knowledge gained can be applied by developing health education programs, facilitating referrals, promoting preventive healthcare, and coordinating with available community resources. Ultimately, the activity highlights the importance of empowering families to actively participate in improving and sustaining their health

Activity 2A

Concept Mapping on Community Health Nursing Concepts

Activity #2 was a 90-minute, face-to-face collaborative learning activity where our group built a concept map with "Community Health Nursing" at the center. The session started with an orientation and a faculty demonstration of concept mapping, followed by group planning and map-making, a group presentation, and a faculty debriefing and reflection. Our concept map connected the required concepts using hierarchical levels, arrows, and linking words, and we explained it in an oral presentation.

Purpose of the Activity- The activity aimed to help us organize, connect, and make sense of the major Community Health Nursing (CHN) concepts in one visual framework. Specifically, it was meant to help us:

  • Apply the concepts, theories, principles, and philosophy of CHN in community-based care
  • Analyze how Primary Health Care (PHC), Universal Health Care (UHC), social determinants of health, levels of prevention, and the roles of the community health nurse relate to each other
  • Organize concepts into a logical, hierarchical, and meaningful map
  • Connect evidence-based practice and current health policies to CHN practice
  • Show critical thinking and teamwork while explaining the map to our classmates and professor.

Skills and Competencies Developed

  • Critical thinking and analysis: explaining how concepts such as PHC, UHC, and social determinants of health affect each other
  • Organizing and synthesizing information: arranging many concepts into clear levels with meaningful links
  • Communication: presenting and explaining the map in a clear and organized way
  • Collaboration and teamwork: planning, dividing tasks, and building one output together within a limited time
  • Research and referencing: using current nursing references and citing them in APA 7th edition
  • Creativity: using colors, symbols, and icons to make the map easy to understand

Application to Community Nursing Practice

  • This activity showed me that CHN is not just about treating illness. It is about seeing the whole picture of a person, a family, and a community. In real practice, a community health nurse can use the family nursing process to assess a household, look at social determinants such as income, housing, and education, and then plan interventions at the right level of prevention. The map also reminded us that community participation and empowerment are needed for health programs to succeed, because people are more likely to support programs they helped create.
  • It also showed how evidence-based practice, digital health tools, ethics, and cultural competence support daily nursing work. Together, these concepts will help us give care that is accurate, respectful, and fair, and that responds to the real needs of the community we serve.

Activity 2B

Video Analysis: The Role of Community Health Nurse in Building Healthy Communities

This activity was a video analysis on the role of the Community Health Nurse (CHN) in building healthy communities. After watching the video, we answered a set of guide questions in six parts: recall and understanding, roles of the community health nurse, application of concepts, Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), Universal Health Care (RA 11223), and critical thinking. We ended with reflection questions about what we realized and how the video changed our understanding of CHN.

Purpose of the Activity- The activity aimed to help us connect what we learned in our lectures to real community health situations in the Philippines. It was meant to help us:

  • Understand the goal of CHN and why health is more than the absence of disease
  • Identify the social determinants of health that affect Filipino families and communities
  • Recognize the different roles of the community health nurse and how they work together
  • Apply CHN concepts to community cases, such as families in flood-prone areas, pregnant women who cannot afford transportation, and elderly patients who cannot afford their medications
  • Link nursing work to the SDGs and to Universal Health Care
  • Think critically about our own future role in our own barangayCommunity

Skills and Competencies Developed

  • Critical thinking: deciding which CHN role or intervention is most important in a given situation
  • Application of knowledge: using SDOH and CHN roles to analyze real community cases
  • Comprehension and recall: picking out the key ideas from a video and explaining them in our own words
  • Problem solving: proposing evidence-based interventions for priority health problems
  • Reflection: looking at our own values and our future role as community health nurses
  • Written communication: answering clearly and in an organized way

Application to Community Nursing Practice

  • This activity showed me that a family's health is shaped by much more than their illness. In real practice, a community health nurse has to look at income, housing, environment, and access to services, and then decide what to do. For example, a family living in a flood-prone barangay may need health education, disease surveillance, and coordination with the LGU, while a pregnant woman who cannot afford transportation may need help with referral and home visits.
  • It also showed that no nurse works alone. Working with BHWs, LGUs, and the HCPN helps bring care to remote communities, and it supports the goals of Universal Health Care and the SDGs. As a future nurse, I now see that my job is to care for patients, teach them, speak up for them, and connect them to the help they need.

Activity 4

Flipped Classroom - Planning and Conducting a Home Visit

This activity focused on planning and conducting a community home visit through the case of the Dela Cruz family. Our group assessed the health condition of Mr. Jose Dela Cruz, a 76-year-old older adult experiencing memory loss, confusion, and possible dementia. We identified several family health concerns, including the risk of wandering, improper medication management, unsafe home conditions, and caregiver stress experienced by his wife and daughter. We also developed appropriate nursing interventions, health teachings, safety measures, and community referrals to address the family’s needs and promote Mr. Jose’s well-being.

Purpose of the Activity

The purpose of this activity was to develop our understanding of how community health nurses assess families in their own homes and identify health problems that may affect their daily lives. It also aimed to help us learn how to plan appropriate nursing interventions, provide health education, promote home safety, and connect families with available community resources. Through this activity, we learned the importance of considering not only the patient’s condition but also the family’s situation, environment, and ability to provide care.

Community Nursing Concepts Demonstrated

  • Home Visit: Understanding how nurses assess the patient’s condition and home environment to identify possible health and safety risks.
  • Health Education: Teaching the family about medication safety, wandering prevention, proper communication, and caregiver support.
  • Environmental Safety: Recognizing household hazards and recommending appropriate safety modifications.
  • Family-Centered Care: Involving family members in decision-making and encouraging them to share caregiving responsibilities.
  • Community Resources and Referrals: Recognizing the importance of coordinating with Barangay Health Workers, the Barangay Health Center, and other healthcare professionals.

Skills and Competencies Developed

  • Improved our ability to assess the physical, cognitive, and environmental needs of an older adult.
  • Developed therapeutic communication skills when dealing with patients experiencing confusion and memory difficulties.
  • Enhanced our critical thinking and decision-making in identifying priority family health problems.
  • Learned how to prepare appropriate nursing interventions and health teaching plans.
  • Improved our ability to provide emotional support and understand the challenges experienced by family caregivers.
  • Developed skills in identifying appropriate community resources and making referrals.

Application to Community Nursing Practice

This activity taught us that community health nursing involves more than assessing and treating a patient’s health condition. Nurses must also understand the family’s situation and the environment where the patient lives. In actual community nursing practice, we can apply what we learned by conducting home visits, assessing household safety, providing health education, and helping families develop simple and realistic care routines. For patients like Mr. Jose, we can teach family members how to communicate calmly, organize medications, prevent wandering, and recognize situations that require medical attention. Most importantly, we learned that nurses should also consider the well-being of caregivers. Providing support, encouraging shared responsibilities, and connecting families with community resources can help make caregiving more manageable.

Activity 5

Evidence Appraisal on Evidence-Based Interventions

This activity focused on reviewing and evaluating a research article entitled “The Effectiveness of Family Health Conversations Delivered by Nurses: A Systematic Review” by Ostergaard et al. (2026). Our group examined how nurse-led Family Health Conversations (FHCs) can help families dealing with critical or chronic illnesses. We discussed the study’s purpose, research methods, major findings, strengths, limitations, and applicability to Filipino families and communities. We also applied the evidence from the article to a Family Nursing Care Plan (FNCP), focusing on improving family communication, emotional support, and shared caregiving responsibilities.

Purpose of the Activity

The purpose of this activity was to develop our ability to understand, evaluate, and apply research findings in nursing practice. It aimed to help us recognize the importance of evidence-based interventions in providing appropriate and effective care to patients and their families. Through this activity, we also learned how to determine whether a research study is credible, identify its strengths and limitations, and understand how its findings can be applied to real situations in the Filipino community.

Community Health Nursing Concepts Demonstrated

  • Evidence-Based Practice: Using research findings to guide nursing interventions and provide appropriate family-centered care.
  • Family-Centered Care: Recognizing the family as an important part of the patient’s recovery and overall health.
  • Health Education: Providing families with information and guidance to help them understand and manage health concerns.
  • Family Health Assessment: Identifying communication problems, caregiving difficulties, and the emotional needs of family members.
  • Health Promotion: Encouraging healthy communication, family participation, and effective coping strategies.
  • Community-Based Nursing Intervention: Applying nurse-led Family Health Conversations during home visits and community health activities.

Skills and Competencies Developed

  • Improved our ability to read, understand, and evaluate nursing research articles.
  • Developed critical thinking skills in identifying the strengths and limitations of a study.
  • Enhanced our understanding of evidence-based nursing interventions.
  • Improved our ability to connect research findings with actual community health situations.
  • Developed skills in preparing a Family Nursing Care Plan based on identified family health problems.
  • Strengthened our communication and teamwork skills through group analysis and discussion.
  • Learned how to recognize the importance of family involvement in managing health conditions.

Application to Community Nursing Practice

This activity helped us understand that nursing interventions should be supported by reliable research and should consider the needs of the patient and the family. The article showed that nurse-led Family Health Conversations can improve the perceived support experienced by families dealing with serious or chronic illnesses. As future community health nurses, we can apply this intervention during home visits by encouraging family members to openly discuss their concerns, understand each other’s needs, and share caregiving responsibilities. We can also provide health education, correct misconceptions, and connect families with available healthcare services when necessary. We also learned that every family has different experiences and challenges, so nurses should not use the same approach for everyone. Instead, we should consider their culture, beliefs, resources, and individual needs when planning interventions. This activity reminded us that providing care means supporting not only the patient but also the people who are involved in their everyday care.

Activity 6

The Dela Cruz Family: Diabetes Management

The activity focused on developing a community-based nursing care plan for a 60-year-old grandmother diagnosed with Type 2 diabetes. The situation highlighted her unhealthy beverage choices, irregular eating patterns, and the importance of maintaining independence in managing her condition. It also addresses the daughter’s role as a caregiver and the need to improve family communication. It involved identifying the family’s health problems, promoting healthy lifestyle practices, providing family health education, applying client-centered care, identifying community resources, and establishing appropriate referral pathways.

Purpose of the Activity

To apply community health nursing principles in managing a family member with a chronic illness. It aimed to develop our ability to assess family health needs, identify health problems, plan appropriate nursing interventions, and encourage family participation in promoting better health and preventing complications of diabetes. It also emphasized the importance of involving family members in healthcare decisions while respecting the client’s preferences, independence, and daily routine.

Community Health Nursing Concepts Demonstrated

  • Family-Centered Care - recognizing that the family is an important part of the client’s healthcare and involving family members in supporting daily diabetes management and healthy lifestyle practices.
  • Client-Centered Care - respecting the grandmother’s autonomy, preferences, and independence in managing her condition instead of imposing health decisions, as this preserves the client’s dignity.
  • Health Promotion - encouraging balanced nutrition, regular physical activity, proper medication adherence, and healthy lifestyle choices.
  • Disease Prevention - promoting blood glucose monitoring, proper foot care, regular checkups to reduce the risk of diabetes-related complications.
  • Family Health Education - teaching the family about diabetes management and appropriate caregiving practices such as recognizing signs of abnormal blood glucose levels, reading food labels, and making healthier food choices.
  • Referral and Continuity of Care - coordinating with healthcare professionals and community resources for appropriate management and follow-up.

Skills and Competencies Developed

The activity enhanced our skills in family health assessment, identifying priority health problems, developing nursing interventions, and providing health education. It also strengthened our communication, active listening, critical-thinking, and decision-making skills. We also developed our communication and interpersonal skills through the application of active listening, empathy, and collaborative decision-making. Furthermore, we developed our ability to promote client autonomy, collaborate with family members, identify available community resources, and establish proper referral plans, and evaluate whether the expected nursing outcomes were met.

Application to Community Nursing Practice

The knowledge gained from this activity can be applied in community nursing practice by conducting family assessments, identifying health risks, and developing individualized care plans for clients with chronic illnesses. As future community health nurses, we can educate families about diabetes management, encourage healthy lifestyle practices, conduct home visits, monitor clients’ conditions, and coordinate with healthcare providers for referrals and follow-up. Most importantly, we can provide holistic and client-centered care by respecting individual preferences and involving families in health-related decisions.

Reflection and Learning Insights

I learned that community health nursing involves understanding not only the patient’s illness but also their lifestyle, preferences, and family situation. Managing diabetes requires realistic changes, proper guidance, and respect for the patient’s choices. One challenge was balancing the grandmother’s health needs with her personal preferences and the daughter’s concerns as a caregiver. I consider both perspectives and focus on client–centered care, open communication, shared decision-making, and practical health education. I certainly will keep in mind these lessons by listening to patients, understanding their needs, and encouraging small, sustainable lifestyle changes rather than simply giving instructions.

Activity 7

Best Practices in Community Health Nursing Care

The activity focused on identifying, discussing, and presenting evidence-based best practices in community health nursing. It discussed three major interventions: community-based childhood immunization education and outreach, the use of an Immunization Management Information System (IMIS), and diabetes self-management education through trained Barangay Health Workers (BHWs). It also explored their supporting research, target populations, implementation, and expected health outcomes in Philippine communities.

Activity 7

Purpose of the Activity

To understand the importance of evidence-based practice in improving community health services. It aimed to develop our ability to identify reliable research, understand effective nursing interventions, and apply research findings to address health concerns such as incomplete childhood immunization, limited access to healthcare, and diabetes management within the community.

Community Health Nursing Concepts Demonstrated

  • Evidence-Based Practice - using research findings to guide nursing decisions and select appropriate community health interventions.
  • Health Promotion and Disease Prevention - promoting childhood immunization, diabetes awareness, and healthy lifestyle practices.
  • Community Participation - encouraging the involvement of families, community leaders, and BHWs in health programs.
  • Health Education and Counseling - addressing misinformation, improving health awareness, and educating families about disease prevention.
  • Community Health Assessment - identifying barriers to healthcare access, immunization, and disease management.
  • Collaboration and Partnership - strengthening cooperation among nurses, BHWs, families, and local healthcare providers.
  • Health Information Management - recognizing importance of accurate health records, immunization monitoring, and proper documentation.

Skills and Competencies Developed

The activity strengthened our research and information analysis skills by requiring us to examine journal articles, identify supporting evidence, and understand how research findings can be translated into actual nursing interventions. It also enhanced our critical-thinking and evidence-based decision-making skills in determining appropriate strategies for different community health concerns. Moreover, we developed our ability to identify target populations, explain intervention procedures, establish expected health outcomes, and recommend practical solutions to existing healthcare barriers. Our communication, health teaching, teamwork, and presentation skills were also improved through the discussion of research findings and their application to Philippine community settings.

Application to Community Nursing Practice

The knowledge gained from this activity can be applied by implementing evidence-based interventions that address the actual health needs of communities. As future community health nurses, we can educate parents about childhood immunization, assist in improving vaccination monitoring and follow-up, and collaborate with trained BHWs in providing diabetes education. We can also use research findings to guide nursing interventions, identify barriers to healthcare access, and evaluate the effectiveness of community health programs. Through these practices, we can contribute to accessible, effective, and quality healthcare services for individuals, families, and communities.

Reflection and Learning Insights

Effective community health nursing should be paired and guided by research and actual community needs. Education, technology, and collaboration with barangay health workers can improve healthcare delivery. Understanding research findings and identifying how they can be applied in communities with limited resources and different health beliefs were challenging. I focused on the studies’ findings, target populations, and recommended interventions to understand the practical value they bring in community settings. From now on, the use of evidence-based information is a standard I will set when providing health education, promoting disease prevention, and improving not just one family’s condition, but to continue working with the community in order to shed light on the small health-related matters that make a major difference in the long run.

General Personal Learning Summary (Lira)

These activities helped me understand that community health nursing goes beyond providing health instructions or managing a patient’s condition. Among the activities we encountered, I realized that caring for someone with diabetes requires more than telling them what to eat or what habits to avoid. It also means that we must understand their daily routines, personal preferences, and willingness to make changes. Family involvement is also just as important, but support should not mean that it takes away a patient’s autonomy or independence. Instead, nurses should advocate and openly communicate and help these troubled families in making health decisions together. I also gained a deeper appreciation for the importance of evidence-based practice in improving community health. The discussions on childhood immunization, the use of the Immunization Management Information System, and diabetes self-management education showed me that effective healthcare demands more than good intentions. It needs a reliable basis of information, proper and concrete planning, accessible services, and cooperation among nurses, barangay health workers, families, and the community. It came to my enlightenment that nurses must be willing to adapt to approaches that would suit the actual needs and limitations of the patients they care for. We must find the best possible ways to achieve better health outcomes for our patients. This obliges us to become someone who listens before giving informed advice, comprehends before making judgements, and provides care that considers each person’s circumstances. Not every problem can be solved immediately, but even small, consistent efforts can lead to meaningful improvements. These lessons encourage me to continue developing both my knowledge and skills, alongside my patience, compassion, and sense of responsibility needed to serve individuals, families, and communities.

Activity 8

E-Portfolio Development

The activity focused on developing an electronic portfolio that compiles and showcases our group’s learning experiences, academic outputs, and understanding of the concepts and principles of Community Health Nursing I. It involved organizing our previous activities, documenting our learning achievements, presenting our reflections, and demonstrating how the knowledge and skills gained throughout the course can be applied to family and community health nursing practice. The portfolio also served as a record of our academic progress and professional development as future community health nurses.

Purpose of the Activity

To consolidate and demonstrate the knowledge, skills, and competencies acquired throughout Community Health Nursing I. It aimed to encourage reflection on our learning experiences, recognize our progress in understanding community health nursing concepts and establish connections between classroom activities and actual nursing practice. It also aimed to develop our ability to organize academic outputs, evaluate our learning, and identify areas for further improvement as future nurses.

Community Health Nursing Concepts Demonstrated

  • Holistic Community Health Nursing - recognizing the importance of addressing the different health needs of individuals, families, and communities through comprehensive nursing care.
  • Family Nursing Process - understanding how family assessment, identification of health problems, planning, implementation, and evaluation contribute to improving family health.
  • Evidence-Based Practice - recognizing the importance of using research findings and reliable information to guide nursing interventions and healthcare decisions.
  • Health Promotion and Disease Prevention - understanding the role of health education, preventive measures, and community-based interventions in maintaining health and preventing illness.
  • Primary Health Care - recognizing the importance of accessible, affordable, and community-oriented healthcare services.
  • Community Participation and Collaboration - appreciating the involvement of families, community members, healthcare workers, and other stakeholders in achieving better health outcomes.
  • Professional Development and Reflective Practice - evaluating personal and group learning experiences to recognize strengths, identify areas for improvement, and develop professional nursing competencies.

Skills and Competencies Developed

The activity strengthened our organizational and documentation skills by requiring us to compile, arrange, and present our academic outputs in a systematic and accessible manner. It also enhanced our reflective and critical-thinking skills through the evaluation of our learning experiences, recognition of acquired competencies, and identification of areas that require further improvement. Moreover, we developed our digital literacy, communication, teamwork, and collaboration skills through the preparation and organization of the electronic portfolio. By reviewing our previous activities, we also strengthened our ability to connect theoretical concepts with practical nursing applications and recognize the importance of continuous learning and professional development.

Application to Community Nursing Practice

The knowledge and competencies gained from this activity can be applied by maintaining organized documentation of nursing activities, evaluating the effectiveness of interventions, and continuously reflecting on professional practice. As future community health nurses, we can use the Family Nursing Process and evidence-based information to assess family health needs, develop appropriate nursing interventions, and evaluate health outcomes. We can also apply the importance of collaboration, health education, and community participation when addressing health concerns. Through continuous learning, proper documentation, and self-evaluation, we can improve the quality of care we provide and become more competent and responsible community health nurses.
